Methods, scope, and limitations

Population and source scope

The saved qualification snapshot contributes 160,092 positive, finite paired-device records across 2,489 distinct wafers and 23 measurement protocols. The export contains 3,527 wafer–protocol summaries. A wafer can occur in multiple protocols. The viewer selects exactly one protocol at a time, defaulting to the protocol with the most eligible paired-device records. This is a saved snapshot, not a live data feed.

Pairing and eligibility

Eligibility requires an unambiguous device identity, a pre-baseline electrical record, and an endpoint recorded by the wafer qualification date. The saved pairing uses first baseline and first subsequent endpoint within the same test-condition group. Both degradation ratios must be positive and finite; 5,404 otherwise eligible rows fail this joint requirement and are excluded. Their absence is not a passing result. Zero endpoints are outside this ratio-analysis population; this report is not a burn-in yield calculation. Date-only qualification records do not establish same-day chronology.

Aggregation and outcome definitions

Each wafer value is the median of eligible device-level endpoint / baseline ratios within one protocol. A scatter point pairs those two wafer medians; it is not an individual device. The two medians may come from different median-ranked devices. The electrical association uses the median pre-stress resistance of the same eligible devices, multiplied by one positive normalization constant across the export.

Recorded qualification = Qualified / (Qualified + Non-pass A + Non-pass B) among selected wafers. The two non-pass labels preserve distinct source groups without exposing internal status codes. These are recorded wafer outcomes, not device-level verdicts. No chip pass counts or burn-in yield are inferred from degradation ratios.

Positive ratios are retained, including extremes. Logarithmic ratio axes make the full range visible. No outlier trimming or invented pass thresholds are applied.

What changed for the public edition

Wafer IDs, product names, equipment identities, protocol names, and calendar dates are replaced or removed. Original identity mappings, server addresses, database fields, source paths, exact recipes, and physical failure-analysis details are not included. New public UI code provides filters, plots, tables, and downloads without a database connection.

Observed values are not synthetic. Positive constant normalization preserves electrical and geometry trends while hiding absolute calibration. Dimensionless device degradation ratios and sample counts remain factual. Relative-week bins preserve chronology at weekly resolution; they do not preserve exact dates or within-week ordering. The interpretation of specific mechanisms has been generalized, without substituting a new causal claim.

Mechanism and next evidence

The public discussion is limited to generic process variation, measurement variation, and cohort differences. Observational associations do not isolate a physical cause. A stronger conclusion would require verified process histories, measurement repeatability, and a controlled test of the proposed mechanism.
